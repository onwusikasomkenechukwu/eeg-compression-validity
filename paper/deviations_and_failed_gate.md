## Pre-registration and deviations

The claim, hypotheses, primary test, stop rule, FP32 sanity gate, latency definition, split rule and analysis plan were registered on OSF before the Stage 0 run [OSF link, registered 2026-10-25; analysis code at commit 3a9b77d]. One patient (chb01) was analyzed beforehand as a pipeline pilot; the decisions made after it (an interpolated primary endpoint, a per-patient specificity check, a 300-epoch cap, latency from raw alarms, and a strict threshold comparison) are listed in the registration. The Stage 0 run was paused once and resumed without changes; two interrupted jobs were discarded and rerun from scratch.

After Stage 0 ran and its registered sanity gate failed, the registration was updated [OSF update, submitted 2026-11-10; code at commit 0bafefa] following an advisor review. The update (1) keeps the failed gate and reports it; (2) declares all Stage 0 results exploratory, since they had been seen; (3) adds a protocol-matched FP32 reimplementation check, with a decision rule fixed in advance that decides only which model later stages use; (4) adds an exploratory analysis of sensitivity under false-alarm caps; and (5) frames simulated 6- and 4-bit results as stress points rather than deployment scenarios, since mainstream microcontrollers have no native arithmetic below 8 bits. The check and the cap analysis were registered before either was run; one job was run beforehand as an error-only smoke test, its metrics not inspected and its output deleted.

After the check failed (below), a second update [OSF update 2, submitted 2026-11-10; code at commit 819d4ad] registered the confirmatory test of H1 with Ahlawat's model before any of its jobs were run in this pipeline: five seeds, run in two batches (seeds 0-2, then 3-4) in one fixed environment, with a pre-specified interim report on seeds 0-2 that makes no decision; a two-part fidelity check (an implementation check against Ahlawat's own code before the run, and the registered specificity check on the operating point after it); and the decision rule of the original registration, stated explicitly (H1 supported if the pooled INT8 excess latency lies above the FP32 seed band and is at least 2 s). It also disclosed two exploratory analyses run between the updates: the reimplementation check repeated with unweighted cross-entropy, and W4A8 and FP16 versions of Stage 0's models built from their saved weights. The Ahlawat run started on 2026-11-11 with the registered code unchanged. One fix was made after registration: the statistics script (scripts/analyze_stats.py) at commit 819d4ad contained a syntax error, two string escapes written as literal line breaks, that stopped it from running at all; it was corrected on 2026-11-11 (commit 58d8708) without changing any computation, and the corrected script reproduces the output of the intended code byte for byte. The training and scoring code used by the run does not import it.

On 2026-11-17, with 236 of the 414 seed 0-2 jobs finished on the laptop, the advisor gave access to a GPU workstation (2 x RTX 4090), and a third update [OSF update 3, submitted 2026-11-17; code at commit 58d8708] restarted the confirmatory run there from scratch: all 690 jobs in one new, fixed environment, decided before any Ahlawat result had been seen (only the runner's log had been read). ## Reimplementation check 1: the registered FP32 sanity gate

The registered gate compared the FP32 reimplementation with EpiDeNet's published CHB-MIT results in EpiDeNet's own units. It required seizures detected of at least 82.3% of usable seizures (EpiDeNet's 8.8% miss rate, doubled) and voted window false positives per hour within a factor of two of EpiDeNet's 2.24 (1.12-4.48). The reimplementation detected 92.6% of seizures (171-172 of 185 across seeds; EpiDeNet 91.2%) but produced 5.83 voted false-positive windows per hour, so **the gate failed**. As registered, this indicates that the reimplementation does not reproduce EpiDeNet, not that the hypothesis is false.

A post hoc investigation (not registered) found no systematic bias in how thresholds transfer from validation to test: median test specificity equals the 0.995 target. False positives were concentrated in a few long seizure-free recordings with sustained high-amplitude non-seizure activity: 1% of test recordings carried 57% of false-positive windows. At a threshold that reproduces EpiDeNet's specificity, the reimplementation detects 80.4% of seizures against EpiDeNet's 91.2%.

The investigation also found that the gate did not compare like with like. EpiDeNet's CHB-MIT protocol trains on all seizure-containing records but one, evaluates on the excluded seizure record, uses no tuned threshold, and never evaluates long seizure-free recordings; this study trained on seizure-free records as well, set thresholds at 0.995 validation specificity, and evaluated on all held-out hours. (EpiDeNet's SSWCE loss was tuned on PEDESITE only; its CHB-MIT table has no SSWCE column, so SSWCE was not part of the comparison.)

## Reimplementation check 2: protocol-matched (registered update)

To compare like with like, the FP32 reimplementation was rerun under EpiDeNet's protocol: for each patient, leave one seizure record out, train on the patient's other seizure records (seizure-free records unused), test on the excluded record, positive window = predicted class (probability > 0.5), 2-of-3 vote on 4 s windows; 138 folds x 3 seeds. Early stopping used a random 25% of training windows, stratified by class, held out from gradient updates. Pass criteria were the registered gate's, unchanged.

The reimplementation detected 95.1% of seizures (175, 178 and 175 of 185 by seed; pass) but produced 15.56 voted false-positive windows per hour (6.03, 24.15 and 16.51 by seed), so **the protocol-matched check also failed**. False positives were concentrated: one patient (chb04) carried 43% of them and three of 414 models carried over half; the median patient was at 5.56 per hour, still above the 4.48 limit. At the window level the reimplementation sits at a more sensitive and less specific operating point than EpiDeNet (voted sensitivity 76.0% vs 68.7%; voted specificity 98.70% vs 99.75%).

As the registered rule requires, the architecture was then rechecked against EpiDeNet's Table I. Every layer matches (filters, kernels, pooling and output shapes), and the operation count at the deployed 4 x 2048 input is 2,053,410 MACs against the paper's 2,053,516 (0.005% apart). With nothing to correct, the check's result stands, and under the registered rule **Ahlawat's model becomes the primary model for later stages**.

The likeliest remaining difference lies outside the architecture: this study trains with cross-entropy weighted by inverse class frequency, which moves the default decision toward the seizure class (more seizures and windows detected, more false positives), whereas EpiDeNet's CHB-MIT loss and class handling are unstated. Other unstated differences are the optimizer and learning rate, filtering, normalization, which 181 seizures EpiDeNet counted (185 are usable here) and the number of repetitions (5 seeds there, 3 here).

An exploratory, post hoc rerun of the protocol-matched check with unweighted cross-entropy (same folds, seeds, early stopping and default decision; disclosed in update 2) tested the class-weighting explanation. It detected 92.6% of seizures (170, 173 and 171 of 185 by seed) and produced 12.18 voted false-positive windows per hour (3.44, 20.62 and 12.50 by seed), so it would also have failed. Window-level voted sensitivity moved to EpiDeNet's (70.6% vs 68.7%) but specificity stayed lower (99.20% vs 99.75%). Class weighting therefore explains most of the sensitivity difference but not the excess false positives, which remain concentrated and vary widely between seeds. The rerun changes no registered decision.

## Consequences for the claims

All Stage 0 results are reported as exploratory, as registered in the update. The primary comparison is paired within each trained model and thresholded identically for FP32 and its quantized versions, so a baseline that differs from EpiDeNet does not by itself bias the FP32-vs-INT8 contrast; but the results describe a detector that does not reproduce the published one, and they are not used to support a confirmatory claim. Within that limit, Stage 0 found no measurable frontier shift for INT8 (pooled excess latency +0.22 s, 95% patient-bootstrap interval 0.00-0.67 s, inside the FP32 seed band of -5.87 to 9.57 s) and a large one for simulated 4-bit (+20.7 s; 24 of 72 patient-seed values never reached FP32's false-alarm rate). Under false-alarm caps of 0.5, 1 and 2 per 24 h, INT8's paired change in seizure sensitivity against FP32 was 0.000 at every cap, while 4-bit lost sensitivity (median paired change -0.20, -0.14 and -0.06). W4A8 and FP16 versions of the same 414 models, rebuilt from their saved weights (every rebuild reproduced the original FP32 scores exactly), separate the two halves of the 4-bit cost: with 8-bit activations, 4-bit weights cost +0.50 s (95% interval 0.00-1.25 s; mixed-model mean 1.36 s) and no sensitivity at any cap, and FP16 matched FP32 (0.00 s). Confirmatory tests of the claim move to Ahlawat's model (OSF update 2, 2026-11-10).

## Confirmatory test: Ahlawat's detector

The fidelity check passed. Part A (Ahlawat's own code reproduces his reported AUROC, and this pipeline's model matches his) passed before the run; part B, the operating point, passed after it: FP32 test specificity pooled over all held-out hours was within 0.995 +/- 0.005 for 87 of 120 patient-seed values (at least 80 required). The misses were concentrated: chb12, chb15 and chb24 fell outside the range for all five seeds, and chb13 and chb22 for four.

**H1 was not supported.** Pooled INT8 excess latency was -0.13 s (95% patient-bootstrap interval -0.30 to 0.00 s; mixed-model mean 0.01 s, -0.18 to 0.21 s), inside the FP32 seed band (-9.91 to 14.93 s, 480 ordered seed pairs) and below the 2 s floor; no INT8 value was censored. 8-bit quantization-aware training (0.00 s) and simulated 6-bit (0.00 s) were also inside the band. Simulated 4-bit was outside it (+16.17 s, 2.79 to 32.75 s; 31 of 120 patient-seed values never reached FP32's false-alarm rate), as in Stage 0. Under false-alarm caps of 0.5, 1 and 2 per 24 h (exploratory), INT8's paired change in seizure sensitivity against FP32 was 0.000 at every cap, while simulated 4-bit's pooled sensitivity fell to 0.30-0.39 against FP32's 0.58-0.66. The share of FP32 test windows near the threshold did not predict a patient's INT8 shift (Spearman rho -0.13 at the registered width), so the H3 prediction did not hold. W4A8 and FP16 versions of the same 690 models, built from their saved weights (exploratory, as registered in update 2), also matched FP32: W4A8 0.00 s (95% interval -0.14 to 0.00 s; mixed-model mean 0.17 s) and FP16 0.00 s, with a paired sensitivity change of 0.000 at every cap. In both detectors, therefore, 4-bit weights with 8-bit activations cost at most 0.5 s, and the large 4-bit cost comes from quantizing activations. Both detectors therefore lead to the same conclusion: INT8 does not measurably move the false-alarm/latency frontier, while 4-bit throughout does.
