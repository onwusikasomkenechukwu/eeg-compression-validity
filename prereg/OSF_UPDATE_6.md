## Justification tab

> Sixth update. The confirmatory test on CHB-MIT is complete (updates 2-5: H1 not supported). Following the advisor's review, this update registers a replication of the same test on a second dataset, Siena Scalp EEG (14 adult patients, 47 seizures; CHB-MIT is mostly paediatric), with the same detector (Ahlawat, 2026), before any model has been trained on Siena. What has been seen of Siena so far: the channel labels, sampling rates, seizure lists, seizure counts and recording hours of every file, and, to check the channel derivation, the per-channel signal spread of one record (PN00-1). No detector output on Siena has been seen. The original registration named a Siena replication with the 8 patients that have 3 or more seizure records as a later stage, exploratory relative to that registration; this update fixes its design and decision rule before it is run, and adds a cross-patient arm.

## Page 6: Analysis Plan → Inference criteria (add at the end)

> Update 6. Replication on Siena. Data: Siena Scalp EEG 1.0.0 (PhysioNet), with the seizure-list corrections listed in the code (two file names, one end time). Input: Ahlawat's 18 bipolar channels (as on CHB-MIT), derived from Siena's referential electrodes by subtraction (T3/T4/T5/T6 for T7/T8/P7/P8; labels matched ignoring case, since the files spell some differently), band-passed causally and resampled as on CHB-MIT; all 41 recordings have every channel. Model, training, quantization (ONNX Runtime INT8; simulated 8-, 6- and 4-bit; 8-bit QAT), thresholds, smoothing, SzCORE scoring and the excess-latency endpoint are exactly those of the CHB-MIT confirmatory test; seeds 0-4.
>
> Two arms, analysed and reported separately, never pooled with each other or with CHB-MIT.
> (1) Primary: the patient-specific arm, the design of the CHB-MIT test. It needs at least 3 records with a seizure per patient, so it covers 8 of the 14 patients (PN00, PN05, PN06, PN09, PN10, PN12, PN13, PN14; 32 folds, 37 seizures, about 82 h), 160 jobs. PN01, PN03, PN07, PN11, PN16 and PN17 have only 1-2 such records and are excluded from this arm. With 8 patients the patient-bootstrap interval will be wide; this is stated in advance and is not grounds to change the design.
> (2) Secondary, added here: a cross-patient arm, the design the original registration named for CHB-MIT (leave one patient group out), applied to Siena, where each patient is their own group (leave one patient out: train on 12 patients, validate on the next patient, test on the held-out one), covering all 14 patients and 47 seizures, 70 jobs. It asks a different question from the patient-specific arm (one model for everyone) and is not a replication of the CHB-MIT design.
>
> For each arm separately: fidelity check B as registered in update 2 (FP32 test window specificity pooled over all held-out hours within 0.995 +/- 0.005 for at least two thirds of patient-seed values: 27 of 40 in the primary arm, 47 of 70 in the secondary); if it fails, that arm is reported as exploratory with the failure. Decision rule as on CHB-MIT: the result replicates a frontier shift if the pooled INT8 excess latency lies above that arm's FP32 seed band and is at least 2 s; otherwise it is reported as a replication of the CHB-MIT finding of no shift. Recall under false-alarm caps of 0.5, 1 and 2 per 24 h is exploratory in both arms, as on CHB-MIT. W4A8 and FP16 versions are built from the primary arm's saved weights (exploratory; the rescoring script supports the patient-specific arm only).
